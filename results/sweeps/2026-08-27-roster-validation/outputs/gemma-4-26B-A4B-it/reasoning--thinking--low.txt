=== CONTENT ===
To find out how many times all three trucks arrive at the same moment, we need to determine the frequency at which their arrival times coincide. This is found by calculating the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of their arrival intervals.

**1. Find the LCM of the arrival intervals:**
*   Truck A arrives every **12 minutes**.
*   Truck B arrives every **18 minutes**.
*   Truck C arrives every **30 minutes**.

We can find the LCM using prime factorization:
*   $12 = 2^2 \times 3^1$
*   $18 = 2^1 \times 3^2$
*   $30 = 2^1 \times 3^1 \times 5^1$

The LCM is calculated by taking the highest power of each prime factor present in the numbers:
*   $\text{LCM}(12, 18, 30) = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 5^1$
*   $\text{LCM} = 4 \times 9 \times 5 = 180 \text{ minutes}$

**2. Convert the LCM to hours:**
*   $180 \text{ minutes} \div 60 \text{ minutes/hour} = 3 \text{ hours}$

All three trucks arrive together every 3 hours.

**3. Determine the total time elapsed and the number of occurrences:**
The timeframe is from 6:00 AM to 6:00 PM, which is a duration of **12 hours**.

We list the times they arrive together starting from 6:00 AM:
1.  **6:00 AM** (Start time)
2.  6:00 AM + 3 hours = **9:00 AM**
3.  9:00 AM + 3 hours = **12:00 PM**
4.  12:00 PM + 3 hours = **3:00 PM**
5.  3:00 PM + 3 hours = **6:00 PM** (End time, included because the range is "inclusive")

Counting these occurrences, we find there are 5 times.

ANSWER: 5

=== CONTENT ===
To find how many times all three trucks arrive at the same moment, we need to determine the frequency at which their individual arrival schedules align.

**1. Determine the alignment interval (Least Common Multiple)**
The trucks return at intervals of 12 minutes, 18 minutes, and 30 minutes. For them to arrive together, the time elapsed must be a common multiple of all three intervals. We calculate the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of 12, 18, and 30.

*   Prime factorization of 12: $2^2 \times 3$
*   Prime factorization of 18: $2 \times 3^2$
*   Prime factorization of 30: $2 \times 3 \times 5$

To find the LCM, we take the highest power of each prime factor present:
$$LCM = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 5 = 4 \times 9 \times 5 = 180 \text{ minutes}.$$

**2. Convert the interval to hours**
180 minutes is equal to $180 / 60 = 3$ hours. This means that every 3 hours, all three trucks will be at the warehouse at the exact same time.

**3. Identify the specific arrival times**
We need to count these simultaneous arrivals between 6:00 AM and 6:00 PM inclusive. We start with the initial arrival mentioned in the problem and add 3-hour intervals until we reach or pass 6:00 PM.

*   **1st Time:** 6:00 AM (The starting moment given in the problem).
*   **2nd Time:** 6:00 AM + 3 hours = 9:00 AM.
*   **3rd Time:** 9:00 AM + 3 hours = 12:00 PM (Noon).
*   **4th Time:** 12:00 PM + 3 hours = 3:00 PM.
*   **5th Time:** 3:00 PM + 3 hours = 6:00 PM.

**4. Verify constraints**
The problem specifies the range is "inclusive" of 6:00 AM and 6:00 PM. Since the trucks' cycle (3 hours) divides evenly into the total time span (12 hours), they arrive simultaneously exactly at the end of the period as well.

Listing the times:

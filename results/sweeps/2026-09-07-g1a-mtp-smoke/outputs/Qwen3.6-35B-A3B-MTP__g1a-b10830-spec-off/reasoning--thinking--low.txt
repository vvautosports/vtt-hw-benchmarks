To find out how many times all three trucks arrive at the same moment, we need to determine how often their individual schedules align. This requires finding the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of their return intervals.

**1. Find the Least Common Multiple (LCM)**
The intervals for the trucks are:
*   Truck A: 12 minutes
*   Truck B: 18 minutes
*   Truck C: 30 minutes

We need to find the smallest amount of time that is evenly divisible by 12, 18, and 30.

*   Prime factorization of 12: $2 \times 2 \times 3$ ($2^2 \times 3$)
*   Prime factorization of 18: $2 \times 3 \times 3$ ($2 \times 3^2$)
*   Prime factorization of 30: $2 \times 3 \times 5$

To find the LCM, we take the highest power of each prime number present in the factorizations:
*   Highest power of 2: $2^2 = 4$
*   Highest power of 3: $3^2 = 9$
*   Highest power of 5: $5^1 = 5$

$\text{LCM} = 4 \times 9 \times 5 = 180$ minutes.

So, all three trucks will arrive together every **180 minutes**.

**2. Convert to Hours**
There are 60 minutes in an hour.
$180 \text{ minutes} / 60 \text{ minutes/hour} = 3$ hours.

The trucks arrive together every 3 hours.

**3. Determine the Number of Occurrences**
*   **Start Time:** 6:00 AM (This is a given arrival time).
*   **End Time:** 6:00 PM.
*   **Total Duration:** From 6:00 AM to 6:00 PM is exactly 12 hours.

Now, we list the times when they coincide (starting from 6:00 AM and adding 3 hours each time):
1.  **6:00 AM** (Start)
2.  6:00 AM + 3 hours = **9:00 AM**
3.  9:00 AM + 3 hours = **12:00 PM** (Noon)
4.  12:00 PM + 3 hours = **3:00 PM**
5.  3:00 PM + 3 hours = **6:00 PM** (End)

The problem states the time period is inclusive of both 6:00 AM and 6:00 PM. Therefore, we count all the instances listed above.

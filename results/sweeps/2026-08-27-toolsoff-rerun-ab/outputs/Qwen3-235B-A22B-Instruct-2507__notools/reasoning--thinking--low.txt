=== CONTENT ===
We are given that three delivery trucks start together at 6:00 AM and return to the warehouse at different intervals:

- Truck A: every 12 minutes  
- Truck B: every 18 minutes  
- Truck C: every 30 minutes  

We want to know **how many times all three trucks arrive at the same time** between **6:00 AM and 6:00 PM, inclusive**.

---

### Step 1: Find the Least Common Multiple (LCM)

We need to find the **LCM** of the three intervals (12, 18, 30) to determine how often all three trucks arrive together.

#### Prime factorizations:
- 12 = 2² × 3
- 18 = 2 × 3²
- 30 = 2 × 3 × 5

Take the highest power of each prime:
- 2² (from 12)
- 3² (from 18)
- 5 (from 30)

So,
> LCM = 2² × 3² × 5 = 4 × 9 × 5 = 180 minutes

Thus, **all three trucks arrive together every 180 minutes**.

---

### Step 2: Total Time Window

From **6:00 AM to 6:00 PM inclusive**, that’s a 12-hour window.

Convert to minutes:
> 12 hours × 60 = 720 minutes

But since we are including **both** 6:00 AM and 6:00 PM, we are looking at **all times from t = 0 to t = 720 minutes**, inclusive.

The trucks start together at **t = 0** (6:00 AM). Then they meet again every 180 minutes.

So the meeting times occur at:
- t = 0
- t = 180
- t = 360
- t = 540
- t = 720

These are all within the interval [0, 720] minutes.

Let’s list them:

| Time        | Minutes from start |
|-------------|--------------------|
| 6:00 AM     | 0                  |
| 9:00 AM     | 180                |
| 12:00 PM    | 360                |
| 3:00 PM     | 540                |
| 6:00 PM     | 720                |

So there are **5** times when all three trucks arrive together **between 6:00 AM and 6:00 PM inclusive**.

Note: The problem says "inclusive", and since 6:00 PM is exactly at 720 minutes and divisible by 180, it **is included**.

---

ANSWER: 5

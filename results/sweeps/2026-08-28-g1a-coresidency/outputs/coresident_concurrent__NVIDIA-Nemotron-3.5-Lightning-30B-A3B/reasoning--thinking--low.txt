=== REASONING ===
We are given: "Three delivery trucks all arrive at a warehouse together at 6:00 AM. Truck A returns every 12 minutes, truck B every 18 minutes, and truck C every 30 minutes. Between 6:00 AM and 6:00 PM inclusive, how many times do all three trucks arrive at the same moment? Show your reasoning, then state the final count on its own line as 'ANSWER: <n>'."

We need to find the number of times from 6:00 AM to 6:00 PM inclusive that all three trucks arrive at the same moment.

First, they all arrive together at 6:00 AM. Then they will arrive together again at the least common multiple (LCM) of their intervals. The intervals are 12 min, 18 min, 30 min. LCM of 12, 18, 30.

Calculate LCM: 
12 = 2^2 * 3
18 = 2 * 3^2
30 = 2 * 3 * 5
LCM = 2^2 * 3^2 * 5 = 4 * 9 * 5 = 180 minutes.

So they arrive together every 180 minutes (3 hours).

Now we need the interval from 6:00 AM to 6:00 PM inclusive. Let's compute the total time span.

6:00 AM to 6:00 PM is exactly 12 hours. But inclusive means we count both endpoints. At 6:00 AM they all arrive together. At 6:00 PM they will also have arrived together if the time is a multiple of 180 minutes from 6:00 AM.

We need to count the number of times t where t is a multiple of 180 minutes, starting at 0 (6:00 AM) and ending at 12:00 PM? Wait 6:00 PM is 12 hours after 6:00 AM. 12 hours = 720 minutes.

We have starting time 6:00 AM as the first arrival. The arrivals occur at times: 0, 180, 360, 540, 720 minutes after 6:00 AM. In terms of clock time: 6:00 AM, 9:00 AM, 12:00 PM, 3:00 PM, 6:00 PM.

We need to count these inclusive from 6:00 AM to 6:00 PM. Let's list: 
1. 6:00 AM (0 min)
2. 6:00 AM + 180 = 9:00 AM
3. 9:00 AM + 180 = 12:00 PM
4. 12:00 PM + 180 = 3:00 PM
5. 3:00 PM + 180 = 6:00 PM

So the times are at 6:00, 9:00, 12:00, 3:00, 6:00 PM. That's 5 times.

Check if inclusive includes 6:00 PM? "Between 6:00 AM and 6:00 PM inclusive" means the interval includes both start and end points. Since 6:00 PM is exactly 12 hours later, and 720 is a multiple of 180 (720/180 = 4), so it is included. So there are 5 times.

Let's double-check: 
Start at 6:00 AM. Then after 3 hours: 9:00 AM. After another 3 hours: 12:00 PM. After another 3 hours: 3:00 PM. After another 3 hours: 6:00 PM. So total 5 moments.

We must "Show your reasoning, then state the final count on its own line as 'ANSWER: <n>'."

Thus final answer: ANSWER: 5

=== CONTENT ===
The three trucks arrive together at 6:00 AM, and they will arrive together again at the least common multiple (LCM) of their return intervals: 12 minutes, 18 minutes, and 30 minutes.

- Prime factorization:
  - 12 = 2² × 3
  - 18 = 2 × 3²
  - 30 = 2 × 3 × 5
- LCM = 2² × 3² × 5 = 4 × 9 × 5 = 180 minutes.

Thus, all three trucks arrive together every 180 minutes (3 hours). The time period from 6:00 AM to 6:00 PM inclusive is exactly 12 hours, which equals 720 minutes. Dividing 720 by 180 gives 4 intervals. Adding the initial arrival at 6:00 AM, the arrival times are:

- 6:00 AM (0 min)
- 9:00 AM (180 min)
- 12:00 PM (360 min)
- 3:00 PM (540 min)
- 6:00 PM (720 min)

This yields a total of 5 times all three trucks arrive at the same moment.

ANSWER: 5

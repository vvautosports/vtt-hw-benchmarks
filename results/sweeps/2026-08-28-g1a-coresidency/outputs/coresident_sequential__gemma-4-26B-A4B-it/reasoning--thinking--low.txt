=== CONTENT ===
To find out how many times all three trucks arrive at the same moment between 6:00 AM and 6:00 PM inclusive, we need to find the least common multiple (LCM) of their arrival intervals.

1.  **Find the intervals of each truck:**
    *   Truck A: 12 minutes
    *   Truck B: 18 minutes
    *   Truck C: 30 minutes

2.  **Calculate the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of 12, 18, and 30:**
    *   Prime factorization of 12: $2^2 \times 3^1$
    *   Prime factorization of 18: $2^1 \times 3^2$
    *   Prime factorization of 30: $2^1 \times 3^1 \times 5^1$
    *   To find the LCM, we take the highest power of each prime factor present in the numbers:
        *   $2^2 = 4$
        *   $3^2 = 9$
        *   $5^1 = 5$
    *   $\text{LCM} = 4 \times 9 \times 5 = 180$ minutes.

3.  **Convert the LCM into hours:**
    *   $180 \text{ minutes} = \frac{180}{60} \text{ hours} = 3 \text{ hours}$.
    *   This means all three trucks arrive together every 3 hours.

4.  **Determine the timeframe:**
    *   The trucks start together at 6:00 AM.
    *   The period is from 6:00 AM to 6:00 PM, which is a total of 12 hours.

5.  **List the arrival times:**
    *   1st time: 6:00 AM
    *   2nd time: 6:00 AM + 3 hours = 9:00 AM
    *   3rd time: 9:00 AM + 3 hours = 12:00 PM (Noon)
    *   4th time: 12:00 PM + 3 hours = 3:00 PM
    *   5th time: 3:00 PM + 3 hours = 6:00 PM

Since the question asks for the number of times they arrive "inclusive" of the start and end times, we count all five occurrences.

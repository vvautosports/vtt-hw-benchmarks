=== CONTENT ===
- Speculative decoding accelerates autoregressive inference by letting a cheap draft mechanism propose tokens verified in a single batched forward pass from the target model, achieving large-model quality near draft-model cost while keeping outputs distributionally identical to standard decoding.  
- The draft mechanism can be a separate small model, an n-gram lookup over prompt and generation history, or a newer multi-token-prediction (MTP) head trained jointly with the target to predict a short window of future tokens from the same hidden state.  
- Draft economics vary by workload: n-gram drafting achieves high acceptance rates almost for free on code and structured text, open-ended prose accepts fewer speculative tokens and a poorly matched draft model can make generation slower than running the target alone because every rejected batch wastes a full verification pass, while MTP heads add a small amount of memory and compute per step but offer stable acceptance across domains.  
- Mixture-of-experts (MoE) models complicate speculative verification, as each token routes to a small subset of experts enabling tens-of-billions-parameter models to decode quickly on integrated GPUs with unified memory, but batching multiple positions together pulls diverse expert weights through memory per step, eroding the batching advantage on bandwidth-bound hardware and raising the break-even acceptance rate compared to dense models.  
- Server-side parallelism requires joint tuning of speculative mode, slot count, and sampling settings, as slot-based KV-cache splitting trades off per-stream speed against aggregate throughput and speculation competes with extra slots for compute headroom, necessitating end-to-end token-per-second measurement on representative tasks instead of microbenchmarks.  

Speculative decoding’s speed and economics are tightly coupled to workload, model architecture, and server configuration, requiring joint tuning and end-to-end measurement on representative tasks rather than isolated microbenchmarks.

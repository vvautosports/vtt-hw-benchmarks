=== CONTENT ===
* Speculative decoding accelerates inference by using a cheap draft mechanism to propose tokens that a target model verifies in a single batch, maintaining the target model's output distribution.
* Draft mechanisms can be small models, n-gram lookups (effective for structured text), or multi-token-prediction (MTP) heads, which offer more stable acceptance rates across domains.
* Poorly matched draft models can slow down generation in open-ended prose because rejected batches waste target model verification passes.
* For Mixture-of-experts (MoE) models on bandwidth-bound hardware, the break-even acceptance rate is higher than for dense models because batched verification pulls more expert weights into memory.
* Server-side optimization requires jointly tuning speculative mode, slot count, and sampling settings to balance aggregate throughput and per-stream speed.

TL;DR: Speculative decoding improves inference speed by verifying draft tokens in batches, but its efficiency depends on the draft method, the workload, the target model's architecture, and server-side configurations.

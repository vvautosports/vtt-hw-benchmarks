=== CONTENT ===
* Speculative decoding uses a cheap draft mechanism to propose tokens that a target model verifies in a single batch, maintaining the target model's output distribution while reducing cost.
* Drafting methods vary from separate small models and n-gram lookups—which excel at structured text—to multi-token-prediction (MTP) heads that offer stable acceptance rates across domains.
* While beneficial, a poorly matched draft model can slow down generation in open-ended prose because every rejected batch wastes a verification pass.
* In Mixture-of-experts (MoE) models, speculative batching can increase memory bandwidth pressure by routing to more experts, raising the acceptance rate needed to be profitable.
* To optimize server-side performance, speculative mode, slot count, and sampling must be jointly tuned and measured via end-to-end tokens per second.

TL;DR: Speculative decoding accelerates inference via draft-and-verify mechanisms, though its effectiveness depends on the drafting method, the workload, the model architecture, and server-side configuration.
